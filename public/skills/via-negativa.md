# Via Negativa

> The fastest way to improve a system is often to remove something from it. Via Negativa is the discipline of finding what to cut.

## What it is

Via Negativa (literally "the negative way") is an improvement method that looks for gains through removal rather than addition. Most teams respond to problems by adding — another feature, another meeting, another step. This skill forces the opposite question: what could we delete?

## When to use it

- A product's feature count has crept past what anyone uses
- Processes keep growing without anyone removing the old ones
- A system has become too slow or too complex to reason about
- You're about to add something "because it doesn't hurt" — it does hurt

## How it works

1. Inventory: list what the system currently does or contains
2. For each item, ask: "If this were removed, who would notice — and would they be upset?"
3. Cut the items that cost more to keep than to remove
4. Re-examine what remains; removal often reveals more removable things

## Example

> A product ships with 47 settings. Via Negativa asks which settings survive the "who would actually notice" test — nine do. Removing the other 38 makes the settings page easier to build, test, and understand, and support tickets about confusing options largely disappear.
