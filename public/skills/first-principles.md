# First Principles

> Stop copying what others do. Decompose the problem to its base truths, then rebuild the answer from there.

## What it is

First Principles is a reasoning method that strips a problem to its most basic, undeniable truths and rebuilds a conclusion from those. It's the antidote to "that's how everyone does it" and "we've always done it this way."

## When to use it

- You're copying an industry convention without knowing why it exists
- Incremental tweaks keep failing to move the needle
- You're pricing, architecting, or scoping something and the reference points don't fit
- You want a genuinely new answer, not a slightly different version of an existing one

## How it works

1. State the problem in one sentence
2. Decompose it: what are the fundamental facts you cannot deny?
3. Question every assumption you inherited from others
4. Rebuild a solution from the fundamentals, ignoring the conventional answer

## Example

> "Batteries are too expensive" — instead of accepting industry pricing, decompose: what are the raw materials (lithium, cobalt, nickel)? What do they cost? What's the theoretical minimum? Rebuild the cost model from there, and the pricing target changes entirely.
